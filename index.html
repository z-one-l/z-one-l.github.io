Zhiyi Li

Xiamen University · Generative modeling for inverse design

I am a student in the Department of Electronic Engineering at Xiamen University. I build IrisFlow, a generative model for the inverse design of optical multilayer structures. It is a practical inverse-design method: engineers in my lab already use it for their designs, and we are working to integrate it into design software. We have used it to solve several design cases without any traditional optimization; the results are in our paper. I strongly believe it can make optical multilayer design much easier.

More broadly, I believe flow matching can drive inverse design and generation in other fields as well, especially problems where discrete and continuous variables are coupled.

Main project

IrisFlow inverse design of optical coatings where the design space is the input

IrisFlow is a generative model for the inverse design of multilayer optical coatings. You hand it a design request: the spectrum you want, the wavelength band you care about, the materials you can actually deposit, given only as their optical curves, and how many layers you allow. It generates a pool of candidate stacks from pure noise, denoising the discrete material choices and the continuous thicknesses together. Every candidate is re-simulated exactly, so you select by whatever rule matters to you and take the recipe to the deposition chamber.

How the two flows steer each other

A coating is a list of layers, each with a material and a thickness, and IrisFlow generates both at once: a continuous flow for the thicknesses and a discrete flow for the materials, under one shared clock. They are not two samplers running side by side. At every step one network reads each layer’s thickness and material together, and what it predicts crosses over: thicknesses reshape the material probabilities, and materials move the predicted final thicknesses.

Try the real model

The panels above are illustrations. This one runs IrisFlow itself: your request goes to a Hugging Face Space, which borrows a GPU for a few seconds, generates several designs and re-simulates each one with the transfer-matrix method (normal incidence, from air onto a fused-silica substrate; layer 1 sits on the substrate). The materials that were never seen in training are given to the model only as their n,k curves. Hugging Face gives each anonymous visitor only a few GPU runs; after that, or when no GPU is free, the request runs on a CPU with at most 8 designs and takes longer. If nobody has used the Space for a while it sleeps, and the first request takes about a minute to wake it.

Paper

IrisFlow is described in the preprint below. I conceived and designed the study, developed the framework, implemented the code, ran the simulations and benchmarks, and wrote the manuscript; my co-authors fabricated the samples and supervised the project.

  1. arXiv
    Joint Discrete-Continuous Flow Matching for Open-Vocabulary Inverse Design of Multilayer Optical Coatings
    Zhiyi Li, Yuheng Jin, Yidan Huang, Nan Chen, Hongyan Fu, and Yikun Bu
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.08392. First author, preprint under review , 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML Website

    Neural inverse design promises to replace per-target optimization of multilayer optical coatings with a single trained model, but three closed-world assumptions built into current architectures block deployment: materials are learned as fixed vocabulary tokens, the wavelength grid is frozen at training time, and layer thicknesses are discretized. Here, IrisFlow, a joint discrete-continuous flow-matching framework, instead receives the design space itself as input. The target reflectance-transmittance spectrum, the wavelength grid, the candidate materials’ optical constants, and the layer count all enter at inference time; material sequences and continuous thicknesses are generated jointly by coupled discrete and continuous flows, with no thickness quantization. Across a 224-task benchmark, a single 136-million-parameter model designs 2-100-layer stacks, holds same-order accuracy on 15 materials never seen in training, reconstructs spectral bands up to 1100 nm beyond its training envelope, reaches oblique-incidence and polarized targets through an input transformation alone, and outperforms an autoregressive baseline on that baseline’s own material library. Designed against optical constants calibrated to the deposition chamber in which they are grown, four colored coolers fabricated by ion-assisted evaporation render their target colors while reflecting up to 95% of the solar near-infrared.
